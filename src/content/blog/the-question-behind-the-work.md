## A place to connect the dots

I’m Iefan, an independent engineer working across software and hardware. My interests include security architecture, embedded systems, decentralized protocols, and intelligence at the edge. This notebook brings those interests into the same conversation.

The question connecting them is simple to state and difficult to answer: how do we build technology that gives people more agency over their digital lives?

It is a question about architecture, but also about experience. A system can claim to give people control while making that control too difficult to use. A protocol can remove one intermediary while quietly introducing another dependency. A device can protect a secret while leaving the person holding it without a recovery path.

## Follow the trust

Security begins with deciding what a system needs to trust. Which component can read the data? Who can authorize a change? What happens when a device disappears, a key expires, or a service becomes unavailable?

These questions often lead below the interface. They lead to key lifecycles, firmware, identity, and communication. They also lead back to the person using the system. A recovery mechanism is a technical construction with a human consequence.

I’m interested in that whole path. The most useful design is often the one whose assumptions can be explained clearly enough to challenge.

## Why the work crosses disciplines

Embedded hardware makes constraints visible. Memory, power, connectivity, and physical access cannot be wished away. Decentralized protocols make coordination and ownership visible. Security engineering makes the consequences of misplaced trust visible.

Putting these perspectives together does not produce a universal answer. It produces better questions. Does the device need a server for this decision? Is a signature enough, or do we also need current authorization? Can a person recover their content without giving someone else permanent control over it?

That is the kind of thinking this publication is intended to hold.
